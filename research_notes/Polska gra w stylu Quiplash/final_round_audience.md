# Quiplash series: final-round variants, audience system, ending, criticisms (reference for an ORIGINAL Polish party game)

> Research-process note: the two main fan wikis (jackboxgames.fandom.com and jackbox.wiki / "Jackboxpedia") block normal page fetches (HTTP 402 / 403 / Cloudflare challenge). The Fandom **series page** "Quiplash (series)" was retrieved in full as raw wikitext via the MediaWiki API (`api.php?action=parse&page=Quiplash_(series)&prop=wikitext`) — the individual Fandom pages "Quiplash", "Quiplash 2", "Quiplash 3" are redirects into it or don't exist. ## 1. Final-round variants: prompts, answering, voting, scoring, point values

### Takeaway
Every Quiplash version replaces the 1-v-1 head-to-head format with a special third round: Quiplash 1/XL = everyone answers one shared prompt and players split 3 votes freely; Quiplash 2 = "Last Lash" (one of three special prompt formats) with ranked gold/silver(/bronze) voting; Quiplash 3 = "Thriplash", which is still **head-to-head (pairs), but each player writes three answers to one prompt**, with tripled points. The request's premise of "matchups of 3" in Thriplash is not supported — sources describe two sets of three answers competing, with an odd player facing the host.

### Cited Findings

**Regular rounds (baseline for scale, needed to understand final-round values)**
- Rounds 1 and 2: each player answers two prompts; each prompt is a head-to-head of two players; players (not the two authors) and audience vote for one of the two answers "or they may not choose at all". — [Fandom: Quiplash (series)](https://jackboxgames.fandom.com/wiki/Quiplash_(series))
- Scoring round 1: "1% of those who voted = 10 points" + 100-point bonus for winning; round 2 values doubled. — [Fandom: Quiplash (series)](https://jackboxgames.fandom.com/wiki/Quiplash_(series)). Consistent with a search snippet of the Quiplash 3 guide: round 1 caps at 1,000 for 100% of votes per prompt, round 2 doubled — [TrueAchievements QL3 guide](https://www.trueachievements.com/game/The-Jackbox-Party-Pack-7/walkthrough/3) (snippet only). One snippet describes the winner bonus as "10% of the given point pool" — numerically the same as 100 of 1,000 (no real conflict).
- "Quiplash" bonus: awarded if all the players and more than half of the audience voted for the quip; reward 250 instead of 100. "Super Quiplash" if more than 90% of the audience votes for it. — [Fandom: Quiplash (series)](https://jackboxgames.fandom.com/wiki/Quiplash_(series)). **Disagreement:** Jackboxpedia (snippet) gives the Quiplash 2 sweep bonus as 100 (round 1) / 200 (round 2) — [Jackboxpedia: Quiplash 2](https://jackbox.wiki/wiki/Quiplash_2). These may be version differences (QL1 vs QL2) or one wiki is wrong; unresolved.
- Achievements define sweep thresholds: "Get a Quiplash (sweep votes in a 5+-player game)" and Super Quiplash = "all player votes and 90% audience votes in a 5+ player game" — [Fandom: Quiplash (series), achievements](https://jackboxgames.fandom.com/wiki/Quiplash_(series)). Suggests sweeps are only possible with 5+ players (with 3 players only one non-author voter exists).
- JINX: identical answers → neither player scores. — [Fandom](https://jackboxgames.fandom.com/wiki/Quiplash_(series)); confirmed for Quiplash 3 by [Netflix Tudum guide](https://www.netflix.com/tudum/articles/jackbox-games-party-essentials-news).
- No answer / censored answer → opponent gets all the points by default. — [Fandom](https://jackboxgames.fandom.com/wiki/Quiplash_(series))
- Safety Quip (introduced QL2): inserts a pre-written answer, worth half points; reworked in QL3 to be "decently related to the prompt", half points still apply; not available in the Last Lash. — [Fandom](https://jackboxgames.fandom.com/wiki/Quiplash_(series))

**Quiplash 1 / Quiplash XL — "Last Lash"**
- All players answer **the same prompt**. Each player has **three votes** which can be stacked on one answer or distributed; each audience member has **only one vote**. All players divide **3,000 points** via "1% of those who voted = 30 points". — [Fandom](https://jackboxgames.fandom.com/wiki/Quiplash_(series)); the 3,000-point pool split by vote share is also in [Jackboxpedia: Quiplash](https://jackbox.wiki/wiki/Quiplash) (snippet: "every player will receive a percentage of the 3,000 point pool, with the answers ordered by vote count").
- **Possible disagreement on audience in this round:** a Jackboxpedia snippet says "the audience's three individual votes are counted in aggregate. A majority of the Audience votes received will be given to their respective players" — [Jackboxpedia: Quiplash](https://jackbox.wiki/wiki/Quiplash). This could mean the audience acts as a combined bloc (like one extra 3-vote voter whose votes follow audience majority) rather than each audience member's vote counting individually as Fandom implies. Could not read the full page to resolve.
- Achievement "He Who Lashes Last…: Get 70% or more of the votes in the Last Lash" — [Fandom](https://jackboxgames.fandom.com/wiki/Quiplash_(series)) (confirms vote-share is the scoring basis).
- Self-voting: not stated for QL1's final round in sources found.

**Quiplash 2 / InterLASHional — "The Last Lash"** (one of three formats chosen per game)
- Acro Lash: "Come up with an acronym for a series of three letters" (prompt gives a theme + 3 letters). Comic Lash: "Finish a comic strip" (fill the final speech bubble). Word Lash: "Come up with a clever sentence that includes a certain word." — [Fandom](https://jackboxgames.fandom.com/wiki/Quiplash_(series)); same descriptions in [Jackboxpedia: Quiplash 2](https://jackbox.wiki/wiki/Quiplash_2) (snippet).
- Voting: players see all answers and choose the three best, awarding **gold, silver, bronze** (ranked); **in 3- or 4-player games only gold and silver**. Audience members have **one vote**. "All players then receive points based on the voting results." — [Fandom](https://jackboxgames.fandom.com/wiki/Quiplash_(series))
- Even in custom-prompt games, The Last Lash always uses one of the three built-in formats. — [Fandom](https://jackboxgames.fandom.com/wiki/Quiplash_(series)); complained about in [Steam: Changing the Last Lash](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1111940/discussions/0/5297878338982043761/).
- Achievement "Those Who Lash Last – Play every Final Round question type" — [Fandom](https://jackboxgames.fandom.com/wiki/Quiplash_(series)); [TrueAchievements](https://www.trueachievements.com/a313218/those-who-lash-last-achievement).

**Quiplash 3 — "Thriplash"**
- Players get a prompt and must give **three answers** to it; "the other players and the audience must decide which set of three are the funniest". In odd-numbered games, the player ranked last after round 2 is matched against the host character, who answers with a pre-written response (even on player-made prompts). — [Fandom](https://jackboxgames.fandom.com/wiki/Quiplash_(series)). Implies **pairs of players**, each submitting a set of 3, voted as a set (one vote per voter per matchup).
- "Instead of one answer, you submit three witty responses to a single prompt"; sweeping the room's votes earns a "massive bonus". — [Netflix Tudum](https://www.netflix.com/tudum/articles/jackbox-games-party-essentials-news); developer page: "three times the chances to be funny" — [jackbox.studio Quiplash 3](https://www.jackbox.studio/games/the-jackbox-party-pack-7/quiplash-3) (via search snippet).
- Points are **tripled** in this round; a guide snippet says the maximum per prompt is **6,000 for 100% of votes**; players can't vote for their own answers; order of the three answers matters for comedic build-up (guide's advice) — [TrueAchievements QL3 guide](https://www.trueachievements.com/game/The-Jackbox-Party-Pack-7/walkthrough/3) (search snippet only). **Ambiguity:** "tripled" relative to round 1 (1,000) would be 3,000; 6,000 implies tripled relative to round 2 (2,000). Only one source gives the number — unverified.
- Achievements: "Schmitty Schmackdown – Get a Quiplash against Schmitty in the Final Round"; "The Best for Last – Got a Super Quiplash in the final round"; "Worst to First – Be in last place at the end of either the First or Second Rounds and then win the game". — [Fandom](https://jackboxgames.fandom.com/wiki/Quiplash_(series)) (confirms sweep bonuses still exist in Thriplash and that comebacks are designed for).

### Inferences
- The series' consistent pattern: final round = higher stakes (2–3× points or a large shared pool) to keep trailing players alive; Quiplash 3 even ships a "Worst to First" achievement to celebrate comebacks.
- Design evolution: QL1 free-split votes → QL2 ranked medals (forces discrimination between answers, reduces vote-stacking on friends) → QL3 back to binary head-to-head but with 3 answers (keeps the familiar, fast, easy-to-read pairwise voting while making the finale feel bigger). A designer can choose: all-vs-all ranked (better for small groups, every voter judges everything) vs pairwise sets (faster reveal, TV-friendly).
- Medals scaling down to gold+silver with 3–4 players is a concrete small-group rule worth copying in spirit (ranked slots ≤ number of non-own answers − 1).

### Gaps
- Exact Last Lash (QL2) medal point values (e.g. points per gold/silver/bronze) — not found in any accessible source.
- Whether Thriplash's sweep bonus amount differs from rounds 1–2; whether jinx applies per answer in Thriplash.
- Whether QL1's final round forbids self-voting; whether the 3,000 pool is fixed regardless of player count.
- Exact handling of audience in final rounds (individual vs aggregated bloc) — conflicting snippets, see above.

## 2. Audience: joining, vote counting/weighting, caps, small vs large audiences

### Takeaway
Audience members join the same room code at jackbox.tv once player slots (max 8) are full or the game has started; up to 10,000 audience members are supported. In the regular rounds the best available evidence (a 2015 Steam thread and a 2016 review) says **each audience vote counts the same as a player vote**, which lets a big stream audience swamp the 3–8 players — a long-standing complaint. Audience can't win points; their input decides sweep bonuses via percentage thresholds (>50% / >90%). No source documents any audience down-weighting ("audience as a fraction of a vote") in Quiplash; that mechanism exists in other Jackbox titles (e.g. Roomerang audience = one vote total).

### Cited Findings
- Audience joins via own phone/browser at jackbox.tv with the room code; up to 10,000 participants; only one person owns the game; extra people can sit in the Audience and rotate in next game. — [PlayStation Blog (2015)](https://blog.playstation.com/2015/06/30/quiplash-new-party-game-expands-audience-participation-to-10000/)
- Quiplash series supports 3–8 players and 10,000 audience. — [Fandom](https://jackboxgames.fandom.com/wiki/Quiplash_(series))
- Players beyond 8 "can still play along as members of the audience"; Jackbox recommends rotating players. Audience can vote in Quiplash, XL, 2, 3; Quiplash 2 / InterLASHional additionally have optional "Audience Play-Along" (audience can submit text); QL3 audience can't submit text. — [Jackbox blog: How audience play-along differs](https://www.jackboxgames.com/blog/how-audience-play-along-differs-in-each-jackbox-game)
- In Roomerang (different game), audience votes collectively "count as one vote (represented by the TV icon)" — [Jackbox blog](https://www.jackboxgames.com/blog/how-audience-play-along-differs-in-each-jackbox-game). Shows Jackbox does use bloc-weighting in some games, but this source does not say it for Quiplash.
- Regular-round points are percentage of *all* voters incl. audience ("1% of those who voted = 10 points") — [Fandom](https://jackboxgames.fandom.com/wiki/Quiplash_(series)); points based on how many players "including the participating audience members" voted — [Giant Bomb](https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Games/Quiplash) (search snippet).
- "Public votes are worth just as much as player votes when it comes to winning" … with 100+ viewers this "makes the 3-4 player votes pretty insignificant"; user asks for a ratio setting; zero replies, no developer answer (2015). — [Steam: Public vote ratio?](https://steamcommunity.com/app/351510/discussions/0/530647080136312408/). A 2016 review likewise says audience votes are worth as much as players' — [Gaming Lives: I Heart… Quiplash](http://www.gaminglives.com/2016/08/12/i-heart-quiplash/) (search snippet).
- Sweep bonus thresholds use audience as a percentage: Quiplash = all players + >50% audience; Super Quiplash = all players + >90% audience. — [Fandom](https://jackboxgames.fandom.com/wiki/Quiplash_(series))
- Final rounds: audience member has one vote vs players' three (QL1) or medal ranking (QL2). — [Fandom](https://jackboxgames.fandom.com/wiki/Quiplash_(series))
- Streamer controls: hosts can hide the room code ("Allow Room Code Hiding") and set a player limit; Twitch "Audience Kit" extension lets viewers play inside Twitch. — [Jackbox blog: charity stream](https://www-origin.jackboxgames.com/blog/playing-jackbox-for-your-charity-stream); [Jackbox support: Audience Kit](https://support.jackboxgames.com/hc/en-us/articles/15794775761047-How-do-I-use-the-Audience-Kit-extension-for-Twitch)

### Inferences
- For a LAN/home Polish game, audiences are small (0–10), so equal weighting is mostly fine; for robustness, a cap such as "audience bloc ≤ X% of total vote weight" (or Roomerang-style "whole audience = 1 vote, split by its percentages") avoids the stream-swamping problem the Steam thread describes.
- Percent-based thresholds (>50%, >90%) for bonuses scale naturally with any audience size, whereas absolute counts would not.

### Gaps
- Whether Quiplash 2/3 changed audience weighting compared with 2015 (no later source found; Jackbox does not document it).
- How audience vote is displayed/shown separately on TV in each version (I recall an audience percentage indicator, but found no source — not asserted).
- Any audience cap smaller than 10,000 or behaviour with very small audiences (e.g. 1 audience member making ">90%" trivial) — undocumented.

## 3. Ending: winner, ties, post-game screens

### Takeaway
Winner = highest total points after three rounds; **ties for first are all declared winners** (no tie-break). Post-game details are thinly documented.

### Cited Findings
- "The player with the highest number of points (including any ties for the highest number) after three rounds is declared the winner." — [Fandom](https://jackboxgames.fandom.com/wiki/Quiplash_(series)); "the player with the most points at the end of the game wins" — [TrueAchievements QL3 guide](https://www.trueachievements.com/game/The-Jackbox-Party-Pack-7/walkthrough/3) (snippet).
- Quiplash 3 has "Victory Smush" winner animations and a "Winner Hand" graphic (asset names on the wiki). — [Fandom](https://jackboxgames.fandom.com/wiki/Quiplash_(series))
- Easter egg: if all players submit nothing (or everything is censored), the host ends the game. — [Fandom](https://jackboxgames.fandom.com/wiki/Quiplash_(series))
- Achievement "Quip Royale – Win two games in a row with all the same players" (QL2 InterLASHional) suggests a "same players / play again" flow. — [Fandom](https://jackboxgames.fandom.com/wiki/Quiplash_(series))
- Open-source clone yodigi7/quiplash's display shows voting results and final game results. — [GitHub yodigi7/quiplash](https://github.com/yodigi7/quiplash) (search summary)

### Inferences
- Shared victory on ties is simple and avoids an anticlimactic tie-break; a designer may still add a light tie-breaker (e.g., most final-round votes) if a single winner is wanted.

### Gaps
- Exact post-game screen sequence (e.g., best-quip highlights, "play again / same players / new players" menu, gallery) — not found in accessible sources.

## 4. Criticisms, house rules, and how clones addressed them

### Takeaway
Documented complaints: audience swamping players in streams; QL2's Last Lash formats (Acro/Word) feel weak and **translate badly** (acronyms with letters rare in the target language, puns on short polysemous English words) — directly relevant for a Polish game; Last Lash ignores custom prompts. Few concrete house rules were found. Clones mostly simplify the final round (flat points per vote, longer timers for many answers).

### Cited Findings
- Audience-weight complaint (stream with 100+ viewers drowns 3–4 players), request for adjustable ratio. — [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/351510/discussions/0/530647080136312408/)
- French players: "the last Lash is way less interesting than everything else"; Acro Lash forces letters "rarely used in french (K,W,Y)" and leads to English words; Word Lash "is clearly based on short english words that can be used in many ways" (e.g. "nut" vs French "noix"); suggestions: option to replace Last Lash with a normal prompt round (like QL1), and use custom prompts in the final; estimate that ¼–⅕ of prompts in other translations were fully rewritten rather than translated. — [Steam: Changing the Last Lash](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1111940/discussions/0/5297878338982043761/)
- Another Steam commenter: Quiplash 2 has "the worst final round in all of the three Quiplashes" — Acro Lash and Word Lash "terrible", Comic Lash "decent but gets repetitive". — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/351510/discussions/0/3003303575726087878) (search summary)
- Small groups: with 3–4 players, QL2 reduces medals to gold+silver — [Fandom](https://jackboxgames.fandom.com/wiki/Quiplash_(series)); sweep achievements require 5+ players — [Fandom](https://jackboxgames.fandom.com/wiki/Quiplash_(series)).
- Odd player counts in Thriplash handled by pitting the last-place player against a host-written answer — [Fandom](https://jackboxgames.fandom.com/wiki/Quiplash_(series)).
- Clone lebecchio/quiplash-clone (3–12 players, host browser + phones via WebRTC, no server): final round pays a flat **200 points per vote**; final voting timer raised from spec's 20s to 30s because 20s "is too short for up to 12 answers". — [GitHub lebecchio/quiplash-clone](https://github.com/lebecchio/quiplash-clone)
- Clone simondiep/quiplash-js: planned (not implemented) final round where everyone answers one prompt and awards gold and silver to answers not their own. — [GitHub simondiep/quiplash-js](https://github.com/simondiep/quiplash-js) (search summary)
- Clone FelixWhitefield/Quiplash-Clone: audience votes on each prompt alongside players. — [GitHub FelixWhitefield/Quiplash-Clone](https://github.com/FelixWhitefield/Quiplash-Clone) (search summary)
- Free-software clones: [quiplibre (hyperdriveguy)](https://github.com/hyperdriveguy/quiplibre), [quiplibre (wyattscarpenter)](https://github.com/wyattscarpenter/quiplibre) — no scoring details found.

### Inferences
- For a Polish game: avoid acronym/single-word-pun finals adapted from English; if using a letter-constraint format, draw letters from Polish frequency tables (exclude Q, V, X; weight common letters), and write prompts natively rather than translating.
- Allow GM-authored/custom prompts in the final round too (a direct fix to a stated complaint).
- Runaway-leader mitigation: escalating multipliers (×1/×2/×3) as in the series, plus percentage-of-votes scoring so a trailing player can win a big final; QL3 rewards "Worst to First".
- Waiting/boredom: Thriplash keeps everyone writing in parallel; pairwise reveals keep each TV beat short. A long all-answers ranked vote needs a longer timer (clone evidence: 30s for 12 answers).

### Gaps
- No Reddit threads were retrieved (search engine returned none); popular house rules (e.g. "write for the audience", inside-joke bans, voting for friends) are not documented in accessible sources.
- No sourced discussion of "inside jokes" or "boredom while waiting" specifically; no source for how commercial competitors (non-Jackbox) address runaway leaders.

## 5. TV screen vs phones per phase

### Takeaway
Architecture is documented (TV = shared display of prompts, answers, vote results, scores; phones = private writing and voting controllers), but a phase-by-phase screen breakdown from a reliable source was not found.

### Cited Findings
- Players and audience use their own phones as controllers via jackbox.tv; the game runs on one shared screen. — [PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/2015/06/30/quiplash-new-party-game-expands-audience-participation-to-10000/)
- Answers are written privately; once all are in (or the timer runs out) quips are revealed; both answers to a prompt are shown and players/audience vote. — [Fandom](https://jackboxgames.fandom.com/wiki/Quiplash_(series))
- QL2 Last Lash: "After the timer ends, the players are shown all the answers", then medal voting. — [Fandom](https://jackboxgames.fandom.com/wiki/Quiplash_(series))
- Clone pattern: host screen = single shared display; phones join by QR or 4-letter code, then write answers and vote on phones. — [GitHub lebecchio/quiplash-clone](https://github.com/lebecchio/quiplash-clone)
- Room code hiding option for streams (code not shown on TV lobby). — [Jackbox blog: charity stream](https://www-origin.jackboxgames.com/blog/playing-jackbox-for-your-charity-stream)

### Inferences
- Typical split implied by the above: TV — lobby/code, prompt + countdown, "waiting for N players", reveal of each matchup, vote tallies/percentages and author names, round scoreboard, winner. Phone — name entry, private prompt + text input (+ safety option), vote buttons (excluding own answer), "waiting" screen otherwise. This matches the existing Party Hub architecture (per-player views via `hub.stateFor(pid)`, `/board` TV page).

### Gaps
- Whether phones mirror answers during voting or only show buttons; what audience phones show; exact TV display of audience share — no accessible source.
